How 3-Phase Motor Current Is Calculated

A three-phase motor's current depends on its power, voltage, power factor, and efficiency.

The calculation estimates the electrical current required to supply the motor's mechanical output while accounting for electrical and mechanical losses.

Horsepower Formula

When motor power is given in horsepower, the estimated three-phase current is:

I = (HP × 746) ÷ (√3 × V × PF × η)

Where:

  • I = motor current in amps
  • HP = motor horsepower
  • V = line-to-line voltage
  • PF = power factor
  • η = motor efficiency as a decimal

The value 746 converts horsepower into watts.

kW Formula

If motor power is already given in kilowatts:

I = (kW × 1000) ÷ (√3 × V × PF × η)

The motor's mechanical output power is divided by efficiency to estimate the electrical input power before calculating current.

Why Does √3 Appear?

In a balanced three-phase system, apparent power is related to line-to-line voltage and line current by:

kVA = (√3 × V × I) ÷ 1000

The √3 factor comes from the relationship between the three phases in a balanced AC system.

This formula uses line-to-line voltage. Using phase-to-neutral voltage with the same formula would produce an incorrect result.

Worked Example: 10 HP Motor

Suppose a three-phase motor has:

  • 10 HP
  • 480 V
  • Power factor = 0.85
  • Efficiency = 90%
I = (10 × 746) ÷ (√3 × 480 × 0.85 × 0.90)

The estimated current is approximately:

11.76 A

This is an estimated operating current based on the assumptions above. Actual motor nameplate current should be used when available.

Power Factor

Power factor describes the relationship between real power and apparent power in an AC electrical system.

A lower power factor means more current is required to deliver the same amount of real power.

For this calculator, enter power factor as a decimal:

0.80 = 80%
0.85 = 85%
0.90 = 90%

Motor power factor changes with motor design and operating load, so use the manufacturer's data when available.

Motor Efficiency

Motor efficiency represents how much of the electrical input power is converted into mechanical output power.

Efficiency = Mechanical Output Power ÷ Electrical Input Power

For example, a motor operating at 90% efficiency requires approximately 1.111 times its mechanical output power in electrical input power.

Motor efficiency is entered as a percentage in the calculator and converted internally to a decimal.

Calculated Current vs. Nameplate FLA

The current calculated here is an estimate. It should not automatically be treated as the motor's official full-load amperage.

When the motor nameplate is available, use the manufacturer's nameplate current for equipment sizing and protection decisions.

The calculated value is particularly useful for understanding the relationship between motor power, voltage, efficiency, power factor, and current.

Motor Current and Operating Load

Motor current changes with operating conditions. A motor that is lightly loaded may draw considerably less current than its nameplate full-load current.

Conversely, a heavily loaded motor can approach or exceed its rated current.

When troubleshooting, compare measured current with the motor's nameplate information and operating conditions rather than assuming the motor should always draw the calculated value.

Field Troubleshooting

If a three-phase motor is drawing unexpected current, investigate the electrical and mechanical system together.

  1. Check the motor nameplate. Verify rated voltage, current, horsepower, frequency, efficiency, and power factor where provided.
  2. Measure all three phase currents. Compare the readings for significant imbalance.
  3. Verify line-to-line voltage. Voltage imbalance can affect motor current and heating.
  4. Check motor loading. A mechanical problem can increase motor current.
  5. Check the power factor. Actual power factor may differ from the assumed value.
  6. Check motor efficiency and operating conditions. These affect the relationship between mechanical output and electrical input.
  7. For VFD applications, check drive parameters. Verify motor data, voltage, frequency, current limits, and motor tuning/configuration.

Common 3-Phase Motor Current Mistakes

  1. Using phase voltage instead of line-to-line voltage. The formula used here is based on line-to-line voltage.
  2. Entering 90 instead of 0.90 for power factor. Power factor must be entered as a decimal.
  3. Forgetting motor efficiency. Mechanical output power is not the same as electrical input power.
  4. Treating calculated current as nameplate FLA. Actual motor nameplate data should take precedence.
  5. Ignoring power factor. Real motors do not behave like purely resistive loads.
  6. Ignoring operating load. Actual motor current depends on what the motor is doing.
